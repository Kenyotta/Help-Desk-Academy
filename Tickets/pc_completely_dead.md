\# Incident Summary



A Customer Support employee reported that their desktop computer would not power on. The user stated the workstation showed no lights, fan activity, or POST beep when the power button was pressed. Because the workstation was unavailable, the employee was unable to answer incoming customer calls, creating an immediate business impact.



\---



\# Business Impact



The outage prevented a Customer Support representative from performing their job duties, causing customer calls to queue and reducing the department's ability to respond to customers.



\---



\# Initial Assessment



Based on the user's description, the issue initially appeared to be a complete hardware power failure.



\### Initial Hypotheses



1\. Failed power supply (PSU)

2\. Failed motherboard

3\. Faulty power cable or power source



\---



\# Investigation



\## Questions Asked



\- Confirmed the wall outlet had been tested with another device.

\- Asked whether the workstation was a desktop or laptop.

\- Verified the assigned asset tag.

\- Confirmed the correct desktop (SD1006) was being referenced.

\- Asked whether there were any lights, fan activity, or POST beeps.

\- Asked whether the monitor displayed a "No Signal" message.

\- Asked whether the monitor had a power indicator light.

\- Confirmed there was only one workstation at the user's desk.



\---



\## Evidence Collected



\- User confirmed there were no lights, fan activity, or beep codes.

\- Wall outlet had already been verified as working.

\- Asset Management confirmed the assigned workstation was SD1006.

\- A successful Remote Desktop connection confirmed the workstation was online.

\- Windows loaded normally through Remote Desktop.

\- User reported the monitor remained completely black.

\- User confirmed the monitor had no power indicator light.

\- Reseating the monitor power cable did not restore functionality.

\# Root Cause



The workstation exhibited symptoms of a complete hardware power failure after standard troubleshooting steps were completed. According to company SOP, the workstation required replacement rather than further hardware troubleshooting.



\---



\# Resolution



\- Confirmed the desktop required replacement.

\- Verified the user required a desktop rather than a laptop.

\- Built a replacement workstation using \*\*Server Imaging\*\* in Computer Deployment.

\- Shipped the replacement desktop using \*\*Rush Priority\*\*.

\- Included a prepaid return label for recovery of the failed workstation.



\---



\# Verification



\- Replacement desktop successfully built.

\- Rush shipment initiated.

\- Return label included.

\- User was provided a replacement workstation to restore business operations.



\---



\# SOP Followed



\*\*Hardware Replacement – Complete Power Failure\*\*



\- Verify complete loss of power after basic checks.

\- Confirm workstation type.

\- Build replacement using \*\*Server Imaging\*\*.

\- Ship replacement through Ship Manager.

\- Include prepaid return label for asset recovery.



\---



\# Lessons Learned



\- Always gather evidence before making assumptions.

\- Verify user-reported symptoms using available management tools.

\- Business impact determines incident priority.

\- Following established SOPs ensures consistent and efficient hardware replacement.

\- Not every hardware issue requires advanced troubleshooting; knowing when to transition to the replacement process is an important Tier 1 responsibility.
